Himalayas Make Me Barf

I did not know India stretched north enough to reach the Himalayas. But it does. Me and my friends took a looong bus ride from the valley up 6,000 feet to a city just below the famous towering mountains. The HIMALAYAS!

The streets are narrow. They were built four hundred years ago, and because of that only designed with people and yaks in mind. But four centuries later and the streets are infested with cars. They zoom up and down the winding mountain roads leaving no room at all for people. It's annoying constantly being honked at. If you're a person get used to walking in the ditch. Or being hit by a vehicle.

This has got to be the most difficult place to drive in the world. Like the rest of India, nobody follows the lanes, but here there are more people mixed in, and steep cliffs on either side of the road, no room for error. Our drivers know exactly how wide their cars are.

We wanted to see the snow capped peaks but didn't have it in us to trek up there. So we hired a driver to take us up and up and up. We teetered along the winding roads, our driver felt more like an F1 driver he was so fast. But I trusted him because he turned a seven hour drive into four hours.

As we drove higher the trees and homes disappeared and were replaced with snow, snow everywhere. There were other people up there with us. Mostly other Indians from south of the country. They have enough money to travel and they chose to come here. Most had never seen snow before. They were so excited jumping around in their snow suits. We drove past them all the way up to the tippy top of the pass, 16,000 feet. The highest I have ever been.

I ran out in the snow in my sandals so Georgia could get this great picture of me. The roads hidden by the rolling white hills, it looked like I wandered up this mountain in just my sandals. So I like that photo. And Georgia did a great job taking it. My poncho also looks great.

The drive down is where I fell apart. I think a mix of the altitude and the winding roads is what really upset my stomach. Our driver got used to me gesturing to the side of the road so we could pull over. Then I would puke off the side of the mountain. It was worth it to see these incredible mountains.

-Alec
