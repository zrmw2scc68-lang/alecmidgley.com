Four Outfits is Plenty

Asia is the perfect place to go super UL. When you are hiking in the wilderness most of your backpack is your sleeping bag, shelter, and food. But when you're urban traveling all you really need is extra clothes. That's it. And so that's all I really took. This is my packing list for 6 countries over the course of two months in SE Asia and India.

Backpack

Patagonia Black Hole 25L. I really love the black hole line. They are extra durable and very simple. One big compartment and I have a nice blue one. On my first trip to India I used a Fjallraven pack but it had problems: it was a bit too small, had a wimpy laptop sleeve, and was separated into two main pockets. The black hole solved most of those problems. The size is perfect and it's just one big compartment, perfect for stuffing items (no packing cubes). The side mesh on the Patagonia isn't durable though, it would be very thought provoking to see what this thing would look like with the uhmwpe grid mesh that Pa'lante uses. I used one carabiner for hanging my pack on beds in hostels.

My fanny pack was my daypack. It was stored in the front sleeve and I would deploy it on days I wanted my camera out around town.

Clothing

Not a lot. Four shirts, three bottoms, one pair of socks, four underwear, and a fleece. That is four outfits, for the most part my pants and long sleeve were used for my travel days. And I could have gone without the socks. I went with injinjis so they would work with my sandals, but even in the cold Himalayas I did not need them.

Shoes

It was so warm sandals is all I needed. Packing shoes can easily use half your space, and SE Asia is warm enough you don't need closed toed shoes. I had my bedrocks and I loved them.

Electronics

I am listing these in the order of how much I needed them: phone, charger, camera, earbuds, laptop, SSD. Phone and charger are the only necessary electronics. It is crazy how in this day and age you can let your phone do all the traveling for you. You can order a cab, get a hotel, plan activities, and get a flight, all on your phone and without talking to anyone. At some point I would really love to do a trip with no phone, because it would force me to talk to locals and get recommendations in a more authentic way, and with paper maps there would be no safety net to get you out of trouble. Something for a different time.

I only brought my laptop because I didn't want to ship it home from New Zealand. But it was really nice to have so I could edit photos on the road. The SSD wasn't necessary for this trip either and was just full of footage from NZ.

I really liked using a different film simulation for each country I went to. It gives a distinct shift to everything in your camera role. Nostalgic negative is nice for the blue oceans of New Zealand and the green rice fields of Vietnam. For India I wanted something warmer, and there aren't a lot of options, so I went with sepia which is bold and can be a headache sometimes.

I carried two extra apple adapters that I could swap out depending on which country I was in. In addition to the US I also had the Australia plug, and Europe, with these three I never had problems in all the 7 countries I visited.

Small Things

Passport, toothbrush, toothpaste, hand sani, bandana, dry bag, luggage lock (for hostels), visa, carabiner.

Items I could've left behind

Last second I left behind my bivy, rain jacket, and towel, and I definitely did not need any of that. I packed a sleeping bag liner, because I thought I'd be sleeping in a lot of gross bedding and wanted to keep away from the bugs, but I didn't need it.
